# General History

A brief account of the origin of the land chronologically.

## Before the war

Before the war the land was divided among major cities. Older cities tended to be more powerful, as they were situated near waterways and other important resources. These cities tended to revolve around trading. Old tensions existed among the upper classes of the cities, but this rarely bubbled up to confrontation. Instead, the upper classes would play to financially hurt those they fought with. This often led to the opportunity for martial confrontation, but after decades of devastating fighting the upper classes of the old cities agreed to abide by a treaty which allowed for the use of champions in single combat as a way of winning and losing land or resources. If two of these aristocrats had a dispute they could not settle, each would find a warrior, decide the terms, and allow the duel to commence.

This form of conflict resolution became a tradition of the old cities. Not only that, but the treaty meant that even lower level aristocrats could fight out their disputes provided they could find a champion. This, combined with the argumentative tendencies of the upper class, led to a constant demand for fighters. This created a niche profession in the old cities. Fighters with a reputation for winning could demand high prices to go into the ring, so many of the fighters began to grow in wealth and fame. As the tradition of the duel became more prevalent, scholars, entrepreneurs, and fighters began to search for ways to give combatants an edge. Because only one weapon was allowed by the tradition of the duel, techniques to shift the form of a weapon during combat were used. The techniques took years to master and required special skills and weapons, but eventually all major fighters were forced to adopt this tactic or be left behind by the competition.

The newer major cities had a very different structure. The first new city to form did so by natural forces. It was formed as a gathering of smaller villages. This city was unarmored and poorly organized initially, but was self-sufficient as far as food and other services were concerned. The "upper class" of this city consisted of the leading families of the villages that came together. An informal council made up of the village heads formed naturally in the beginning, and was later written into the city charter. After a while it was decided that a president was necessary, but the president had no real power and mostly just guided council meetings. Over time this first new city began to grow. Because of its loose social structure and lack of formal organization, it became known as a city of opportunity, causing others to flock to the city. Seeing this city's success, other cities began to form under the same format. Because these cities were so new, their organization, power, and size was proportional to how long they had existed.

Smaller cities and villages also existed. Most of these were simple affairs, places that farmed or similar. Most of these places were near rivers or close to cities so as to easily trade.

## The disparity

Following the First Great War, the old societies were unable to sustain themselves. The land was not fertile enough and the livestock populations were decimated. Those that remained alive in the old cities were left stranded, milling around without any idea what to do or where to go. A few individuals realized the situation and left quickly, within days, after the cessation of the fighting. Some of the early leavers were determined to make their own way in the world. These individuals would try to find a few followers to leave with, hoping to take advantage of the chaos to form a nearby settlement and control resources.

Others who left quickly were headed for other cities for news of relatives. These people would often take their families and whatever supplies they could for the trip. For the first few weeks these people would often form caravans to more easily navigate the long trips. As the caravans began to dwindle in size, they became easy targets for raiders who were driven by desperation.

These raiders were often orphans of the war or street rats who were left with no means of survival. Preferring to strike at night and get away unseen, the raiders became known as shadowmen. They formed their own bands and stuck close by one another. As the years went on, travelers became more sparse and were more cautious besides. There were no more caravans of easy pickings, so the bands started to focus on the growing settlements to survive. This was a difficult task with so many eyes around. Only the most adept thieves were sent in to plunder settlements. Using the techniques they learned from the early days of the bands, these shadowmen were able to move through shadow, eventually reaching a point that they were entirely invisible. The ability to move through the shadows without being noticed was of high priority to the bands, so they began to develop a set of techniques that allowed them to do so. Eventually, members became so adept with their shadow play that they were able to use their own shadows to escape sight.

The raiders continued on, some of them reaching the level of being fabled for their talents at moving invisibly, even in broad daylight. Their skills were well known amongst the old aristocracy, who had retained sizeable wealth after the war and were again vying for power. With the tradition of the duel gone, they began to search for more direct ways of gaining power. As such, many of the old aristocracy began hiring the shadowmen to gain information and for assassination missions. With limited business, the bands began competing for jobs. To stay competitive, many turned their skill with shadow manipulation into a martial art, using it to disguise blades and even learning how to use shadow to attack from range. Eventually the shadowmen became known not as thieves anymore but as assassins, causing people to start calling them shadow reavers. The fierce competition drove the bands, once friendly with one another, apart. Due to their reputation, the reavers could not associate with normal people, so the only interaction the reavers had was with one another. These together gave the shadow reavers their other title: the lonely brothers.

In the mercurial political landscape of the disparity, the shadow reavers were about the only constant presence. Figures would rise and fall, but there would always be darker work to be done. Many of the brothers began to feel hollow about their work. Some bands or individuals disappeared into the night, off into the wildlands of the disparity in search of a different life. Many times these people would find a village to call home and settle down. In those villages the inhabitants told stories of spirits who would protect villagers from roving beasts and unsavory humans.

Other bands were forced to leave after failing a mission or being caught in the act. Even in failure, the brothers rarely answered for their mistakes since they would easily evade capture or escape captivity. Bands that lost their favor with the elite would often simply leave to find work as mercenaries, freelancing assassins, or bounty hunters.
