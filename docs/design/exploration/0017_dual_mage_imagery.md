# 0017 — The Dual mage: where she could come from

## The brief (the owner, 2026-10-14)

> Give me some interesting imagery from around the world and characters / classes from other
> games we can use here to make this class interesting along the lines of melee / battle /
> celestial-balance. The idea of a gish in ttrpgs and the battle mage were interesting ones,
> and I didn't fully explore those yet.

What she is today, in one line: two forces, one per hand, that fill two bars as she fights up
close; she vents them in bigger and bigger spells, and overflow lifts her into ascension. The
flavour so far has been "light and dark". The material below is meant to widen that into
something with a silhouette, and each entry ends with what it could *do* in this game, since
an image only earns its place if it makes a mechanic easier to read.

---

## 1. The gish and the battle mage: the problem other designers had

A **gish** (tabletop slang, from the githyanki fighter-wizards of early Dungeons & Dragons) is
a character who fights with a blade *and* casts. Every edition has struggled with it for the
same reason this kit has: casting and hitting both want the character's turn, so a gish is
usually half as good at each. The interesting answers are the ones that **fold the two into
one action**.

| Source | The fold | What it suggests here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pathfinder's Magus** | *Spellstrike*: a touch spell is cast **into the weapon** and goes off when the blade hits. *Spell Combat*: attack and cast in the same turn, at a penalty. | The spell rides the auto. Press `F`/`R` and the spell does not go out -- it loads the **next ball**, which carries it and detonates on contact. The auto stays the thing that aims; the spell becomes its payload. |
| **D&D 5e Eldritch Knight** | *War Magic*: cast a cantrip, then a free weapon attack. A **bonded weapon** that can be called back to the hand. | A spell that ends in a free auto -- or an auto ball that can be **recalled** (the Kalista hop's mirror: the ball comes back to her). |
| **D&D 5e Bladesinger** | The *Bladesong*: a trance in which she adds her intellect to her defence and speed. It is a **dance**. | A stance, not a spell: while held, her hops are longer and her autos faster, at a price on the bars. The ascension already is this; a smaller version could be the middle click. |
| **D&D 3.5 Duskblade** | *Arcane channeling*: a full attack where **every** hit carries the spell. | The Flurry dash, read this way: every blow in the cascade carries whichever spell she last loaded. |
| **Hexblade** | Curses a target and is stronger against that target. | Already hers (the dark curse); worth leaning on harder -- the cursed target is the one her autos home on. |

The lesson from all five: **the spell is either a payload, a stance, or a free follow-up.**
She has spells that go out on their own (0015) and autos that fill bars (0016). What she does
not yet have is one of the three folds, and the Magus's (spell into the blade) is the one
that most suits "a flurry of hits that come from the beings inside them".

## 2. Video-game battle mages built on two gauges

These are the closest relatives, because each one solved "fill by hitting, spend by casting".

- **Final Fantasy XIV's Red Mage** -- the nearest thing to her that exists. Two gauges, *white
  mana* and *black mana*, filled by alternating light and dark spells. Let them drift too far
  apart and the gain stops; keep them level and they unlock a three-hit **melee** combo that
  spends both equally, ending in a finisher of whichever colour was *lower* -- Verholy or
  Verflare. *Steal*: the finisher is graded by the **weaker** bar, so balance is rewarded at the
  moment of cashing, not punished all the time.
- **World of Warcraft's Enhancement Shaman** -- *Maelstrom Weapon*: every melee swing may add a
  stack; at five stacks the next lightning bolt is instant. Fill-by-hitting, spend on a spell,
  with a hard cap. *Steal*: her `Q`/`E` already are this; the cap as a **visible count of
  spheres orbiting her** reads better than a bar.
- **Hollow Knight** -- the Knight's nail strikes fill a vessel of *soul*; soul casts spells or
  heals. The vessel is drawn as a white mask that fills with liquid. *Steal*: show the bars on
  her body, not the HUD.
- **Guild Wars 2's Weaver** -- an elementalist who holds **one element in each hand**; the skill
  on each key changes with the *pair*. *Steal*: the middle click's spell could depend on which
  hand she last used, so twilight is dusk or dawn depending on the order.
- **Diablo II's Assassin / Diablo III's Monk** -- *charge-up* strikes store charges that a
  finisher releases (Tiger Strike, Phoenix Strike); the Monk's *spirit generators* and
  *spenders*. *Steal*: a "charged" auto is a visibly different ball (a ring around it per
  charge), so the opponent can read the danger.
- **Path of Exile** -- *Cast on Melee Kill*, *Spellslinger*: spells socketed to fire when a
  blow lands. *Steal*: the Magus idea again, from the other side.

## 3. Battle mages with a silhouette

Characters to borrow *pictures* from.

- **Rellana, Twin Moon Knight** (*Elden Ring: Shadow of the Erdtree*). A knight with a sword of
  fire in one hand and frost glintstone in the other; her sorcery calls **two moons, one full
  and one dark**, that crash together. Her sword arcs leave crescent trails. This is almost
  exactly the brief: a duellist whose two hands are two celestial bodies.
- **Diana, Scorn of the Moon** (*League of Legends*). A crescent-arc blade swing and **three
  orbiting spheres** that burst on contact (Pale Cascade). Her rival **Leona** is the sun:
  shield, sword and a Solar Flare from the sky. The pair is the sun/moon split already worked
  out as two characters.
- **Yone** (*League of Legends*). Two swords, one for the body and one for the soul; his soul
  **leaves his body**, fights, and snaps back to it. *Steal*: Phase as a soul that goes out and
  comes home, dragging what it hit.
- **Vergil** (*Devil May Cry*). *Summoned Swords*: spectral blades that appear around him and
  fire in sequence -- a cascade. *Judgement Cut*: a slash that happens **at a distance**, a
  sphere of cuts where he looked. Direct reference for the Flurry dash's look.
- **Bayonetta**. Her combos end in *Wicked Weaves*: a giant fist or heel from the demon she is
  bound to, made of her own hair, punching out of a portal. "A flurry of hits that come from the
  beings inside them" -- this is the cleanest existing picture of it.
- **Persona** and **JoJo's Bizarre Adventure**'s *Stands*: a figure that stands behind the
  fighter and does the hitting (JoJo's "ora ora ora" barrage is the flurry). *Steal*: her two
  beings as two half-visible figures at her shoulders, one dark, one light, whose fists *are*
  the auto balls.
- **Destiny's Dawnblade**. A warlock who throws a **sword of fire from the sky** and glides on
  burning wings. A spell-caster whose super is a melee weapon.
- **Final Fantasy V's Mystic Knight** (Spellblade): imbues the weapon with a spell for the
  fight. The original Magus, twenty years earlier.

## 4. Myth and art: one body, two forces

### South and East Asia

- **Ardhanarishvara** (Hindu). Shiva and Parvati as **one body split down the middle**: one half
  male and ascetic, one half female and adorned. The most literal "dual mage" in world art --
  her silhouette could be asymmetric, dark side and light side, rather than her spells being.
- **Nataraja** (Hindu). Shiva as the dancer whose dance destroys and renews the universe,
  standing in a **ring of flame** (*prabhamandala*), one foot raised, one hand holding the drum
  of creation and one the fire of destruction. *Ascension's pose*: she rises inside a ring, and
  the ring is the bar overflowing.
- **Durga** (Hindu). Ten arms, each holding a **weapon given by a different god**, riding into
  battle. "Beings inside her" made visible: every blow of the flurry is a different arm.
- **The Sudarshana Chakra** (Hindu). Vishnu's spinning **disc** with serrated edges, thrown and
  returning to his finger. The Disk ray's sun-disk could be *thrown* like this rather than
  firing a beam -- the same shape, a different verb.
- **Taiji / yin-yang**, and the martial arts built on it: **Taijiquan** (soft yielding into
  hard striking) and **Baguazhang** (walking a circle, changing palms, every strike from a
  turn). *Steal*: her hops as a circle-walk around the target; the middle click as a "palm
  change" that swaps which hand is dark.
- **Nezha** (Chinese). A child-god standing on two **wheels of wind and fire**, with a ring of
  the universe thrown as a weapon. Mobility drawn as two spinning wheels under the feet --
  the two bars as wheels.
- **Xianxia sword immortals** (Chinese fantasy). Flying swords, and **sword arrays**: dozens of
  blades in a ring that fire in sequence. The cascade, again.
- **Fudō Myōō** (Japanese Buddhist). The immovable wisdom king: a sword in one hand and a
  **lasso** in the other, wreathed in flame; fierce because compassionate. A pull (the lasso)
  beside the strike.
- **Raijin and Fūjin** (Japanese). The thunder god inside a **ring of drums**, the wind god with
  a bag of wind. *For Chain lightning*: the cloud as a ring of drums, each bolt a drum struck.
- **Onmyōdō** (Japanese). The "way of yin and yang" of court magicians: paper talismans, the
  five-pointed seal. *For the sigils*: drawn as talismans on the floor.

### The Near East, Egypt and the Mediterranean

- **The eyes of Horus** (Egyptian). His right eye is the **sun**, his left the **moon** (the
  moon eye was torn out by Set and restored, which is why the moon waxes and wanes). Two hands,
  two eyes: her light hand is her right, her dark hand her left, as a rule the art keeps.
- **Ma'at's scale** (Egyptian). The heart weighed against a feather. A balance with a judgement
  at the end of it: the Equinox is a weighing.
- **Ra and Apep** (Egyptian). The sun's boat sails the underworld every night and fights the
  serpent of chaos; an eclipse is Apep winning for a moment. *The black sun* (her dark major)
  already is this.
- **Mithras** (Roman mystery cult). The bull-slaying god, painted with **Sol and Luna** in the
  two upper corners watching. A fighter framed by the two lights.
- **Apollo and Artemis** (Greek). Twin sun and moon, both archers. **Hecate** with two torches at
  the crossroads.
- **Ahura Mazda and Angra Mainyu** (Zoroastrian). The cosmos as a contest of light and dark that
  a person *chooses between* -- which is what the hands are.

### The Americas and the North

- **Hunahpu and Xbalanque** (the Maya *Popol Vuh*). Hero twins who outplay the lords of the
  underworld in a ball game and rise as the **sun and the moon**. A *ball game* -- her autos are
  balls.
- **Huitzilopochtli and Tezcatlipoca** (Aztec). The sun-warrior and the "smoking mirror" of
  obsidian and night. *For the dark hand*: an obsidian mirror rather than purple smoke.
- **Sól and Máni** (Norse). Sun and moon driven across the sky, chased by the wolves **Sköll**
  and **Hati**; at the end of the world the wolves catch them. Two pursuers: the bars as two
  wolves closing on each other.

### Christian, alchemical and astronomical

- **The Ophanim** -- the "biblically accurate angels": **wheels within wheels**, rimmed with
  eyes, never turning as they move. *For the sun-disk*: rings inside rings with beads on the
  rim is already this picture; turning the rings against each other finishes it.
- **Saint Michael** weighing souls with a scale in one hand and a sword in the other: balance
  and violence held at once.
- **The Rebis** (alchemy). The "double thing": one crowned body, half sun-king, half
  moon-queen, holding compass and square, the result of the Great Work's union of opposites.
  Ascension as *coniunctio*: when the two bars meet, they become a third thing.
- **The Ouroboros**. A ring that eats itself -- the bar that overflows into ascension and
  drains back.
- **Tarot**. *Temperance* pours liquid **between two cups** (the Equinox, exactly); *the Chariot*
  is driven by a black and a white sphinx pulling the same way (her two forces as momentum);
  *the Sun*, *the Moon*, *the Star* and *Justice*.
- **A total eclipse**. The black disk and its white **corona**; **Baily's beads** -- points of
  light on the rim the moment before totality; the **diamond ring**. The sun-disk's beads are
  Baily's beads; the black sun's corona is the corona. An eclipse is the one moment light and
  dark are *both* at full and in line: a picture for "both bars high and equal".

### Bodies in motion

- **The Sufi whirling dervish** (the *sema*). The right palm turned **up** to receive from
  heaven, the left turned **down** to give to the earth, turning on one foot. Light from above,
  dark into the ground -- the pose for her twilight spells, and a spin as a move.
- **Kalaripayattu** (Kerala). One of the oldest martial arts, taught with knowledge of the
  body's vital points (*marma*), which a strike can close. *For the curse*: stacks as marked
  vital points drawn on the body.
- **Capoeira**. The *ginga* -- a constant rocking step that never stops -- with kicks disguised as
  dance. Her hops between autos as a rhythm rather than a dash.

## 5. What I would take, if it were mine to choose

Ordered by how much they would change how she plays, most first.

1. **The Magus fold: spells loaded into the next ball** (Pathfinder, Path of Exile, Mystic
   Knight). `F` and `R` stop firing outward and instead *arm* the hand; the next auto from that
   hand carries the spell and goes off where it lands. Every spell is aimed the way the auto is,
   which removes a whole line of effect from her kit, and it is exactly "the beings inside her
   throw the spell". Her `Q`/`E` majors stay cast outright, so there is still a spell that goes
   out on its own.
2. **The Red Mage finisher: graded by the weaker bar** (FFXIV). Cashing out (`Q`/`E`, now the
   only early cash-out) pays more the more **level** the bars are. The razor's edge stops being
   only "how full" and becomes "how full and how even", and the Equinox gets a reason beyond
   itself.
3. **The beings, drawn** (Bayonetta, Stands, Durga, Ardhanarishvara). Two half-visible figures at
   her shoulders whose fists are the balls; they grow as the bars fill and stand fully formed in
   ascension. Costs nothing in rules -- it is the bar drawn on her body (Hollow Knight) -- and it
   is what makes the flurry read as theirs, not hers.
4. **Eclipse / syzygy as a state** (astronomy, Rebis, Rellana's twin moons). When both bars are
   high and within a small margin of each other, she is *in eclipse* for as long as she keeps
   them there: a corona round her, and her next major is both forces at once. A third reward
   between "balanced" and "overflowing".
5. **The Sudarshana throw** (Hindu, Diana). The sun-disk, thrown spinning, cutting through what
   it passes and coming back to her hand. The same disk the ray already draws; a second verb for
   it.
6. **Yone's soul** (League). Phase as a soul that goes out ahead and **snaps back**; she can
   leave her body somewhere safe and fight a moment from where the soul stands.

Pictures alone, worth doing whichever of the above is chosen: Horus's rule (light is always her
right hand, dark always her left), the ophanim rings turning against each other on the
sun-disk, the obsidian mirror for the dark hand, the dervish's palms for the middle click, and
Nataraja's ring of fire for ascension.

## Open questions

- **DUAL-13.** Is she one being with two forces, or a body carrying two beings? The second
  (Bayonetta, Stands, Durga) is what the 0016 brief describes; it decides whether figures are
  drawn at all.
- **DUAL-14.** Does the Magus fold (spells ride the next ball) replace `F`/`R` firing outward, or
  is it an option on them (tap to load, hold to cast)?
- **DUAL-15.** Should cashing out pay for *balance* (the Red Mage) as well as fullness, now that
  the bars no longer drain on their own?
- **DUAL-16.** Which mythology, if any, should lead? A single source (Egyptian sun and moon,
  Hindu dance, Chinese taiji) gives a consistent look; a mix gives more to borrow but no face.
