# 0003 — What the Elementalist's new moves look and feel like

0002 gave the grid and the dispatch tables. This is the other half: what a player sees from
the seat, what the opponent sees from across the arena, and the one moment each move is built
around. Timing words are the ability spec's — fast, medium, slow — and the class's shape holds
throughout: **the cost is paid up front, the payoff is big, the recovery is moderate.**

## Fissure — middle click, standing

She drops to one knee and drives both hands into the floor. That is the wind-up, medium
length, and it is the same pose Landfall borrows today, so a player already knows it. On the
frame it fires, a crack opens at her hands and **races** away along the ground toward the
point the crosshair picked — a line of split earth with light coming up through it, fast
enough that the opponent gets one decision rather than a walk. It runs through anyone it meets
and stops there, staggers them, and a stone heaves out of the floor at their feet. Where it ran,
the ground stays broken and slow for a few seconds: a scar you can see and should not stand in.

The feel target is a *whip crack along the floor*. The heavy commitment is the kneel; the
payoff is that the arena now has a stone where the opponent was standing and a corridor they
would rather not cross. Against a stone in its path it does not stop: it runs under it and
heaves it a metre into the air, the same pop a stone gets when one erupts beneath it, so a
Fissure through your own field tosses your cover at them.

**From the air** she throws the same two-handed drive downward and the crack begins where the
shot lands, running onward away from her along the aim. Coming down on to a stone it *sinks*
it a step instead of heaving it. It is the same move; the situation moves where it starts.

## Lift — first thumb button, standing

She raises one hand, palm up, and the air over the crosshair's point visibly **gathers**: dust
and loose grit begin to rise in a column about a body wide, for a medium wind-up, so anyone
standing in it knows. Then it goes — a hard updraft, a single gust rather than a lasting wind.

What it does depends on what was in it. A stone lifts clean off the floor, rises a couple of
body heights, hangs for a beat, and falls. In that beat it is *earth in the air*: the beam
kicks it down on to somebody, Cataclysm shatters it into debris falling from above, she can
jump on to it, or she can leave it to drop on whoever walks under. A fighter is lifted **by
their own weight** — the Bulwark's boots barely leave the ground, the Dual mage goes up a
storey — so the same cast reads differently on each class and the opponent's weight becomes
something you see. A fire pillar caught in it stretches taller and thinner. Cast on her own
feet it does nothing, on purpose: her second jump is the stone, not a free gust.

The feel target is *a hand under the world*. The composition moment: Raise under your feet,
jump, and Lift the stone as it erupts so it catches you a second time.

## Downdraft — the same button, airborne

Rule 2 from 0002, applied as a single sign flip. The same raised palm, turned down, and the
column is the same column drawn falling. A lofted stone becomes a **meteor** on the spot,
driven straight into the floor and hitting by its speed. A grounded stone is pressed down into
the ground and leaves the slow field where it was. A jumping fighter is spiked. And a fire
pillar is *flattened* — the tall narrow column collapses outward into a ring of fire across the
floor, low and wide, which is the archive's Ring of fire found by a verb rather than given a
button.

Feel target: *slamming a door from above.* The opponent's read is her hovering with a hand
pointed down and a column of falling air drawn under it; the answer is to step out of it.

## Grip — second thumb button, both rows

Press with the crosshair on a stone and it **tears loose** from the floor and hangs in front
of her at a fixed distance, tethered to the crosshair by a visible line of dust. Move the mouse
and it follows, at that distance, at the crosshair's height — up over their head, down at the
floor, wherever she looks. She is committed while she holds it: she crawls, she cannot jump or
dodge, so a floating stone and a slowed mage is a telegraph anybody can see. Release and the
stone is thrown along the line, hitting by speed. Any hit on her drops it where it hovers, and
it falls like a boulder.

While it hangs, every other verb applies to it up there. The beam kicks it early — a fast
throw before she has finished placing it. Cataclysm shatters it over their head — the shotgun,
from above. Downdraft drives it into the floor as a meteor. And a stone pulled in under her
own falling feet is **footing**, which is the one thing about Grip that is different in the
air and needs no second move.

Feel target: *the stone is clay*. The risk is that it reads as too fluid in third person; the
fixed carry distance and the tether are what keep it legible.

## Fire pillar on a stone — no new button

Cast the pillar where a stone stands and the stone **catches**: it glows at the seams and
smoulders, and stays lit as long as a pillar would burn. An armed stone is a bomb waiting for
a shove. The next thing that moves it hard — the beam's kick, a Grip throw, a fall off a Lift
— makes it burst on impact: burning debris instead of plain, and a small fire patch where it
landed. It is one dispatch row on a built move, and it is the earth-plus-fire sentence the
loadout has been asking for.

## Cinder rain — `Q`, airborne

The pillar's opposite shape. She throws a handful of fire *down* rather than planting a column,
and embers fall in a cone under the aim, arriving as a wide, shallow patch of burning ground
that lasts a short time. Where the pillar is tall, narrow and long-lived — the thing that stops
a jump — this is low, wide and brief: area denial from above that says *not here* to a patch
of floor rather than *not through here* to a line. Embers falling on a stone arm it. Embers
falling through a Gale below her light it.

Feel target: *scattering coals.* It is the least verb-like thing in the proposal and the one I
would prune first if the air row needs to be shorter.

## Air bolt and Gale, given tables — built moves changed

The Air bolt is still the long poke. The one addition: a stone it meets is **lofted** rather
than stopping it — a small hop, a body height, not Lift's column — because in the air her
kick is vertical. Through a fire pillar it comes out as fire, the row the kit already lists as
open.

The Gale changes more. It is still the frisbee that opens as it flies and hits harder wide,
but now it **lifts what it passes over**: bodies by their gravity, stones outright. Thrown low
across the floor it is a wave that takes the field up with it — stones and fighters rising as
the disc goes under them and falling as it passes — so a low Gale followed by Downdraft is a
meteor shower you made from your own cover. Thrown through a pillar it comes out burning, a
firestorm disc, the archive's whirlwind-plus-fire.

## Gather — `F`, standing and airborne

Unaimed, so it is about her. Press and every stone she has **tears free and comes to her**,
orbiting at chest height a couple of metres out, slowly, dust trailing. They are still stones:
they block shots and bodies as they do on the floor, so it is cover that walks with her. The
beam kicks one out of the orbit along the line — a ready projectile without placing it first.
Cataclysm shatters one at point blank, a shotgun in every direction. Press `F` again and they
drop where they are, hitting by their fall.

It is a mode, and the cost is that the field is empty: nothing to stand on, nothing to jump off,
the cap spent on herself. Airborne, the orbit rises with her and a stone under her feet is a
floor, so she can hover on three slots' worth of stone. Feel target: *the field folds up around
her.* The question is whether it is a decision or a panic button.

**Sink**, the quieter alternative on the same key: her oldest stone goes back into the floor,
no frames, leaving the slow field. Frees the cap on purpose, and drops whoever was standing on
it.

## Tremor — `R`, standing

Quake, cast at her own feet, which is why it is unaimed. She stamps, and the floor around her
**shakes** for a slow, visible wind-up: a ring of cracked, jittering ground a few metres across.
Anyone *moving* through it staggers; anyone standing still is fine, which is what makes it a
read rather than a bubble. Then it erupts — damage and a stagger to everyone in it but her —
and a stone comes up **under her**, carrying her with it. It is the structure jump with a
telegraph attached: a way to make space and gain height when somebody has closed on her, at
the price of a wind-up they can see and a slot. Standing on a stone already, it pops that one
instead.

Feel target: *the ground says get off me.*

## Hover — `R`, airborne

Press and her fall is **arrested** — a puff of air under her, a hang of a few frames, the same
hang an aerial gives but with no attack attached. Hold and it extends, toward a cap, drifting
slowly with the stick. It spends the airdodge, and she is committed while she hangs: no dodge,
no jump, so a hovering Elementalist is the most readable target in the game. What it buys is
the frames to line up a Gale, a Downdraft, or a Landfall from the height she chose. Release,
or the cap, and she falls.

Feel target: *a held breath.* The cap on the hang is the whole tuning question.

## Blast — the prune alternative for a thumb button

If Grip is cut, this takes its place. A short, fast cone of air from both hands. On the ground
it kicks **every** stone in the cone forward at once — the beam kicks one; this kicks the field
— and shoves bodies back a step. And it shoves *her* the other way, a backstep off the recoil.
In the air, aimed down, that recoil is a **rocket jump**: fire at the floor and go up, fire at a
stone and it goes down while she goes up. It composes with Landfall (blast up, come down hard)
and with the structure jump, and it is the most emergent thing on the list, which is exactly
why it is in the prune section rather than the grid: a rocket jump that is too good becomes
the class.

## Reading the whole thing from the other side

From the opponent's seat, every new move has one of three tells, and it matters that there are
only three: **a kneel** (Fissure, and Landfall's drive), **a raised hand with air gathering
under it** (Lift, Downdraft, Hover, Gather), and **a stamp with the ground shaking** (Tremor,
and the rise of any stone). Grip is its own tell — a floating stone. A class with nine verbs
and three wind-up shapes is one an opponent can learn to read, and that is the readability
budget 0001 said was the real limit.
