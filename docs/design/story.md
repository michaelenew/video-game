# Story — written plots, rolled across the valley

[world.md](world.md) §7 put the story off: "the world has a premise and no more."
This document opens that thread. It proposes a shape and then storyboards six
plots in it, so the shape can be judged on examples rather than in the abstract.

**Nothing here is built, and nothing here is needed for the arenas as they
stand.** Every plot assumes the valley of world.md §2 — places joined by trails,
with Hearth at the mouth — because a story needs places to happen in.

---

## 0 · The shape

Two well-known ways to tell a story in a game, and why neither is the one:

- **A written plot**, one path through the whole world. Every beat in its place,
  so every beat can be good. It costs the most writing, it plays the same the
  second time, and it fights the world's own rule that *no fight is required in
  particular* (world.md §3): a plot that needs you at the Mire next will be
  waiting there while you go and fight the Sandmaw instead.
- **Pure dice** — a generator that makes up events. Cheap per event, endless,
  and nearly always forgettable, because nothing a generator writes was meant.

**The middle is short written plots whose stages are placed by dice.** This is
how *Wildermyth* works (written events, cast with whoever is in your party), and
the general idea is called *storylets* or *quality-based narrative* (the term
comes from *Fallen London*): small written pieces that each say when they are
allowed to happen, and a picker that chooses among the ones that are. Searching
either name finds the long version.

Here, concretely:

| Word | Means |
| --- | --- |
| **Thread** | One written plot: three to five beats, read in order. Six are storyboarded below. |
| **Beat** | One stage of a thread: something the player finds, sees or is told, in one place. |
| **Slot** | The *kind* of place a beat may happen in: "any fight place of tier 2–3", "any trail", "Hearth", or rarely one named place. Written by hand, with the beat. |
| **Binding** | Which actual place fills each slot in *this* save. Rolled once, when the save begins. |
| **Trigger** | When a beat becomes live: the previous beat seen, plus usually a trophy count. Written by hand. |
| **Dressing** | A beat that changes only what is in the place: a prop, a mark, a line from somebody in Hearth, a page of the hunter's notes. Most beats are dressing. |
| **Twist** | A beat that changes the fight itself: an extra defended thing, a creature already wounded, a pack where there was none. Rare, and held to the rules below. |

**What the dice decide**, once per save:

1. Which threads are in this world: **the spine, plus three of the other five**.
   A second save meets different people.
2. Which place fills each beat's slot. The Brother's camp is in the Pinewood in
   one save and on the Saddle in another.
3. Small written alternatives inside a beat — which of three names, which of two
   props. Cheap variety, written once.

**What the dice never decide**: what happens, in what order, or how it ends.
Those are written.

### The rules

1. **A story never changes whether you can win.** It is world.md §1's thesis
   restated: nothing a thread gives you is power, and nothing a thread does to
   a fight makes it out of reach. A twist changes what the fight is *about*,
   not how hard it is — if it adds something (a cart to defend), it is tuned to
   sit inside the creature's own band.
2. **A beat is never in your way.** Beats wait in their place; you can walk
   past. No beat locks a waystone, a trail or a creature.
3. **One twist per visit.** If two threads bind twists to the same place, the
   later one waits for the next visit there.
4. **Every beat is told by the place, a prop, or one line.** No cutscenes, no
   dialogue trees. A person in Hearth says one or two lines and goes back to
   what they were doing. This is what keeps the cost of a thread near the cost
   of an arena variant, and it is the art thesis's "spend writing last" kept.
5. **A twist travels like a temper.** Anything that changes a fight is decided
   before the hunt starts, sent in the picker's message, and kept in the
   snapshot, so both peers build the same fight and a rollback replays it
   (world.md §6, W2). Dressing never touches the simulation at all.

## 1 · The spine — *The Walk* (always present)

The one thread every save has, because it is the ending the world already has:
the Siegeshell walking to Hearth's wall. The question it answers is the one the
fight leaves open — **why is it walking here?**

**The answer:** Hearth's wall is built of shell. Long before the town, in the
old cities' time, a Siegeshell was brought down at the valley's mouth, and the
first settlers built from what was left. The one walking down the Long Valley
is coming for it. It is not attacking the town. It is going to its dead.

| # | Slot | Trigger | What the player sees |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hearth | start | The wall. Its stones are curved and ridged, like no quarried stone. Nobody remarks on it. |
| 2 | any fight place, tier 1–3 | 2 trophies | A curved, ridged slab half-buried in the arena — the same stuff as the wall — and the place's creature avoids it. (Rolled: which arena gets the slab.) |
| 3 | any trail | 4 trophies | Tremors underfoot, every so often, from up the valley. The herd, if you visit it after this, grazes facing *away* from the Long Valley. |
| 4 | Hearth | tier 4 waystone lit | The bells (world.md §3). The bell-ringer, one line: "It came once before. That's what the wall is." |
| 5 | the Long Valley | the fight | During the walk, the Siegeshell lowers its head toward the wall each time it stops. Its trophy, the anchor shard, is the same ridged stuff. Hung in Hearth, it sits beside the wall it matches. |

**Cost:** one slab prop, one wall material, one line, one idle head-lowering on
the Siegeshell. **Rolled:** only beat 2's place.

## 2 · *The Lonely Brother*

From the 2016 history ([archive](../archive/lore/general-history.md)): the
shadow reavers who grew sick of the work "disappeared into the night, off into
the wildlands … In those villages the inhabitants told stories of spirits who
would protect villagers from roving beasts." This is one of them, living in the
valley.

| # | Slot | Trigger | What the player sees |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | any fight place, tier 1–2 | 1 trophy | The creature has an old wound: a clean diagonal scar, drawn on its hide. Nothing else. |
| 2 | any trail | beat 1 seen | A small camp, cold. A blade sharpened down to almost nothing. A shadow on the rock wall that does not match anything casting it. |
| 3 | Hearth | beat 2 seen | A child in Hearth, one line: "The shadow-man keeps the gnawers off the far farms." Nobody else admits to it. |
| 4 | a fight place you have **already beaten**, tier 2–3 | 6 trophies | **Twist.** You return to an old fight and somebody is already in it: the sparring bot, as a Reaver, fighting your creature beside you. It leaves the moment the creature falls. |
| 5 | any trail | beat 4 seen | He is waiting. **A duel**: the sparring bot, Reaver, at the top of its personality range ([sparring.md](sparring.md)). Win or lose, he goes; a Reaver's broken blade appears among your trophies, and the camp is empty from then on. |

**Cost:** a scar decal, a camp prop, one line, and the sparring bot placed into
a hunt (beat 4) and a trail (beat 5). The bot exists; giving it a place to stand
in a hunt is the only new machinery. **Rolled:** every place, and which creature
wore the scar.

**Why it is a good test of the whole idea:** the payoff is a fight, and the
fight already exists. A thread whose climax is a versus match against the bot
costs almost nothing to stage.

## 3 · *The Rival's Ledger*

The old cities settled disputes by hiring champions ([champion.md](champion.md);
the 2016 history). One family still keeps the tradition: a claim on the valley is being argued
out by trophy count, and you have been entered without being asked.

| # | Slot | Trigger | What the player sees |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hearth | 1 trophy | A clerk with a ledger: "You're counted. So is she." Two columns. One is yours. |
| 2 | Hearth (wall of trophies) | each time the rival "wins" | A second set of trophies hangs opposite yours. The rival takes a creature every so often — **rolled** which one, always one you have not beaten yet. |
| 3 | any fight place you have **not** beaten | rival ahead by 2 | You arrive and the creature is already hurt and angry — the rival was here and left. **Twist:** the creature starts with its strain thresholds part-used (a temper-like share, sized so the fight is no easier and no harder, just shorter and meaner). |
| 4 | any trail, past the tier 3 waystone | 7 trophies (either side) | Her camp. Her class is **rolled at save start** and shown here: a hammer on a rack, or a shield, or a staff. |
| 5 | Hearth, by the wall | 9 trophies, or the Siegeshell's bells | **The settling duel.** The sparring bot as the rolled class, in front of Hearth's gate. The ledger is closed whatever the result; the clerk writes one line in it. |

**Cost:** a clerk, a ledger prop, a mirrored trophy rack, a camp. **Rolled:**
the rival's class, which creatures she takes, and in what order — so the rival
is a different opponent every save. **Note:** the rival's count is fiction; she
never takes a creature away from you.

## 4 · *The Caravan*

Also from the 2016 history: after the war, families "formed caravans" to reach
other cities "for news of relatives", and the caravans dwindled. This is one
that came up the valley a long time ago and did not come back down, and a
second, now, following it.

| # | Slot | Trigger | What the player sees |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hearth | 1 trophy | A family loading a cart. One line: "My father's cart went up thirty years ago. We're going to find where." |
| 2 | the crossing (the Hornback escort) | beat 1 seen | **Twist** that already exists: the escort fight is *their* cart. If it arrives, they thank you by name; if it does not, they turn back to Hearth and the thread ends at beat 2b. |
| 3 | any fight place, tier 2–3 | beat 2 won | An old cart wheel, half buried, painted the family's colour. The **rolled** place is where the first caravan stopped. |
| 4 | any trail, tier 3–4 | beat 3 seen | The old cart itself, overgrown, against a rock face. A box in it with a letter. One line of it is legible. |
| 5 | Hearth | beat 4 seen | The family again, around the letter. They are staying. Their cart becomes part of Hearth — a stall by the gate, from now on. |
| 2b | Hearth | the escort lost | The family, unloaded, staying in town. "Next year." The thread can be **offered again** next save. |

**Cost:** two carts (one new, one ruined), a wheel, a letter, two lines.
**Rolled:** where the first caravan stopped, and which of three letters it left.
**Why it is here:** it is the one thread with a real branch, and the branch is
a fight the game already has.

## 5 · *The Collars*

The Pair's trophy is "two collars" (world.md §4). Somebody collared two big
cats. This thread is who.

| # | Slot | Trigger | What the player sees |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | any trail, tier 2–3 | 3 trophies | A cage, broken open from inside, too big for anything you have seen. A brass plate on it with a family crest. |
| 2 | Hearth | beat 1 seen | A notice on the gate with the same crest: a reward for two cats "returned unharmed". |
| 3 | the Den (the Pair) | beat 2 seen | **Dressing only:** the Pair's collars are drawn, with the crest. The fight is the fight. |
| 4 | Hearth | the Pair beaten | The notice is gone. In its place, a man wearing the crest, one line, looking at your trophy: "Those were mine." That is all he says. He stays in Hearth for the rest of the save, by your trophies. |

**Cost:** a cage, a crest, a notice, a collar decal, one character with one
line. **Rolled:** where the cage is, and which crest. **Why it is here:** it is
the cheapest thread of the six and the one with an uncomfortable ending, to
check that four beats is enough to land a feeling.

## 6 · *The Spreading Brood*

The one thread driven by **what you leave alone**. The Broodmother makes more of
the fight as it goes ([broodmother.md](creatures/broodmother.md)); this is that,
at the scale of the valley.

| # | Slot | Trigger | What the player sees |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | any trail near the Hollows | the Hollows' waystone tier reached | Webbing on the trail's edges. Husks of sacs, hatched. |
| 2 | any fight place, tier 1–2, already beaten | 3 hunts later, Broodmother unbeaten | **Twist:** broodlings in an old fight — the same critters the Broodmother hatches, as a small pack beside that place's creature. Tuned, as rule 1 says, to keep the fight in its band. |
| 3 | Hearth | beat 2 seen | A farmer, one line: "There's webs in the low meadow now." |
| 4 | the Hollows | the Broodmother beaten | The webbing goes. Any place that had broodlings is clear on the next visit. The farmer, one line, is back to complaining about the weather. |

If you beat the Broodmother before beat 2 fires, the thread ends quietly at
beat 1: the husks stay, nothing spreads. **Cost:** webbing dressing, broodlings
placed in another species' arena, one character. **Rolled:** which old fights
get broodlings. **Why it is here:** it is a plot made by the player's own order
of play, which is the thing a fixed plot cannot do and pure dice cannot make
mean anything.

## 7 · *The Last Pilgrim*

The Mantis "fights the way a player does" ([mantis.md](creatures/mantis.md)).
This thread says why: it learned from people. The last of them is walking up the
valley now, one place ahead of you.

| # | Slot | Trigger | What the player sees |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | any trail, tier 1–2 | 2 trophies | Footprints in a line that does not stray, and a small cairn with a sword laid on it. |
| 2 | any fight place, tier 2–3 | beat 1 seen | Marks on the arena floor of a duelling circle, freshly scuffed. The creature here has a shallow cut you did not give it. |
| 3 | any trail, tier 4 | beat 2 seen | A second cairn. The sword is gone from it; there is a hammer instead, laid the same way. |
| 4 | the Shrine (the Mantis) | the Mantis fight | On the Shrine's floor, the pilgrim's sword and hammer, broken. **Dressing only** in the fight. After it, the hunter's notes for the Mantis carry one extra line: the move it answered the pilgrim with. |

**Cost:** two cairns, two weapon props, a duelling circle decal, one notes line.
**Rolled:** the three places. **Why it is here:** it is the thread with no
person in it at all — told only by things left behind, which is the cheapest
kind of telling and the test of whether the valley can carry a story by itself.

## 8 · What the six have in common, and what they cost

| Thread | Beats | Twists | New characters | Climax is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Walk (spine) | 5 | 0 | 1 | the Siegeshell (exists) |
| The Lonely Brother | 5 | 1 | 1 child + the bot | a duel with the bot (exists) |
| The Rival's Ledger | 5 | 1 | 1 clerk + the bot | a duel with the bot (exists) |
| The Caravan | 5 (+1) | 1 | 1 family | the escort (exists) |
| The Collars | 4 | 0 | 1 | a line in Hearth |
| The Spreading Brood | 4 | 1 | 1 farmer | the Broodmother (exists) |
| The Last Pilgrim | 4 | 0 | 0 | the Mantis (exists) |

Every climax is a fight the game already has, or a line. That is the point of
the choice: **the writing is the arrangement, and the arrangement is cheap.**

A save carries the spine plus three, so about seventeen beats over eleven
creatures and their trails — something new in most places, without every place
being about a plot.

## 9 · What it needs built, in order

None of this until the valley (world.md §2) has places with exits and a Hearth.
Then:

- **S0 · The roll.** A save records its threads and bindings, the way trophies
  are recorded (`platform.rs`, not the snapshot). One seed, rolled at the start.
- **S1 · Dressing.** A place can carry props and decals named by a beat. The
  beat's state (seen or not) is kept with the save. No simulation change.
- **S2 · Lines in Hearth.** A person standing somewhere says one or two lines
  when you walk near. Shown as text.
- **S3 · Twists.** A beat's twist is a byte in the picker's message, like the
  temper's two bits, and the snapshot carries it. The first three twists above
  are: the bot in a hunt, a creature started part-strained, and another
  species' critters as a pack beside the place's creature.

S0–S2 tell four of the seven threads in full (the Walk, the Collars, the
Pilgrim, and the Caravan, whose twist is the escort that exists). S3 is the
other three.

## 10 · Open

| ID | Question |
| --- | --- |
| STORY-1 | **Three threads per save, or a different count?** Fewer leaves more places quiet; more risks every place being about something. |
| STORY-2 | **Coop: whose story?** The simplest answer is the host's world is the stage and the guest watches it. Whether a beat seen as a guest counts as seen in your own save is open. |
| STORY-3 | **Are duels against the bot good climaxes?** Two of six threads end in one. It is cheap, and it puts versus inside the world, but the bot has to be good enough to be somebody. |
| STORY-4 | **Twist band.** Rule 1 says a twist keeps the fight inside the creature's band. The harness can measure that the way it measures tempers; whether every twist must be measured before it ships is the question. |
| STORY-5 | **Does a thread end for good, or come back next save?** The Caravan's failed branch comes back. Whether others do — and whether a finished thread is ever offered again — is open. |
| STORY-6 | **The spine's answer.** "It is walking to its dead" makes the last fight sad rather than heroic. That is a choice about the whole game's tone, and it is yours. |
